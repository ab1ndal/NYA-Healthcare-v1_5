Structural Ingenuity

Making Stanford’s complex patient towers a reality

Stanford’s vision called for four patient towers cantilevered nearly 30 feet beyond their supporting columns, creating demanding structural and vibration challenges.

NYA had to carefully balance the weight of the projecting floors, control vibration, and integrate the towers with the hospital’s seismic system. Through advanced analysis and engineering judgment, NYA developed a multi-story steel framing solution that met these competing demands.

For Stanford, the result was the realization of its ambitious vision without compromise.

Architectural Vision

Engineering Stanford’s vision for a brighter, more welcoming hospital.

Stanford’s atrium welcomes patients and families with daylight and open space. To achieve the nearly 120-foot skylight without bulky framing or columns, NYA designed a shallow grid of slender steel pipes. Advanced analysis allowed NYA to keep the structure light while meeting demanding engineering requirements, delivering the openness Stanford envisioned.

Seismic Leadership

Helping Stanford keep caring for patients after an earthquake

NYA drew on its pioneering seismic isolation expertise to design a system that supports Stanford’s goal of uninterrupted care. By reducing shaking within the hospital, the system helps protect patients, staff, and medical equipment. NYA’s technical expertise brought that protection into the design, reducing the risk of damage and disruption.

Site Ingenuity

Engineering a nearly million-square-foot hospital on a very small site

UCSF envisioned a major new hospital on a steep, triangular site surrounded by existing buildings, roads, and underground utilities. The limited footprint left little room for the large, flexible floors essential to modern healthcare.

NYA engineered a way to make every square foot count. Dramatic cantilevers extended usable floors beyond their supporting columns, while an innovative structural system accommodated shifting floorplates as the tower rose. Together, these solutions maximized the space available within the site’s restrictive geometry.

The result was a structural design that made UCSF’s nearly million-square-foot hospital possible on a site that seemed far too small to accommodate it.

Architectural Freedom

Making UCSF’s bold architecture possible through innovative seismic design

UCSF needed to fit an architecturally ambitious hospital onto a severely constrained site while meeting California’s demanding seismic requirements. Conventional seismic systems would have occupied precious space and imposed significant restrictions on the building’s design.

NYA engineered an innovative system combining steel moment frames with nearly 200 fluid viscous dampers. By dissipating earthquake energy without relying on restrictive walls or bracing, the system accommodated the building’s dramatic geometry and preserved its open, flexible spaces.

The result was a hospital designed for powerful earthquakes, with the architectural freedom and usable space UCSF envisioned.

Collaboration

Helping UCSF build more efficiently through two California hospital firsts

UCSF saw an opportunity to simplify construction through prefabricated MEP racks and bathroom pods. But making these innovations work required multiple engineering disciplines to resolve competing requirements before construction.

NYA brought the MEP teams together to coordinate shared racks and engineered the structural supports needed to accommodate them. This coordination also minimized MEP clashes before construction. NYA also coordinated the building structure to receive prefabricated bathroom pods.

The result was two California hospital firsts, making installation more efficient, reducing rework, and lowering construction costs for UCSF.
